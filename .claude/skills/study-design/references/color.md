# Color and signaling

Color is a quiet signaling channel, never decoration and never the only carrier of meaning.

The default page uses a warm paper surface and dark neutral ink. One desaturated blue-gray accent anchors links and the main identity. Semantic roles may use subtle variations, but saturation stays low:
- concept/definition → muted blue-gray;
- example/application → muted olive-green;
- warning/exam caveat → muted ochre;
- factual error/high-risk misconception → muted brick;
- conceptual relation → muted slate-violet;
- recall/self-test → neutral graphite/blue-gray.

The textual label (`Definición`, `Ejemplo`, etc.) must remain visible. Semantic callouts are normally transparent with a thin rule; full tinted backgrounds are exceptional. Do not invent subject-specific meanings such as "red = CPU" or "green = memory".

If a screenshot looks colorful before it looks calm and readable, reduce color. Do not add fake paper texture, noise, grain or decorative aging.

Maintain sufficient luminance contrast. The paper tone must never make body text low-contrast.
