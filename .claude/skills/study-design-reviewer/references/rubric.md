# Visual review rubric

## Hierarchy
Can a student infer the document structure in 15–20 seconds? H1, H2, H3, prose and supporting material must have unmistakable roles.

## Readability
Body type, measure, leading and paragraph spacing support sustained reading. Flag lines that feel too long/short, cramped or excessively airy.

## Density
The page should primarily look like a calm contemporary technical manual / university textbook. Flag card soup, repeated boxes, oversized chrome, excessive labels, strong shadows, loud sidebars and empty space that disconnects related concepts. If the design announces itself before the prose, fail it.

## Signaling
Semantic color/rails must guide attention selectively and remain visually quiet. If the page looks colorful, signaling has failed. Meaning must survive grayscale/color-vision differences through labels and structure. Full tinted boxes should be rare.

## Figures
Technical figures are large enough to inspect, visually close to their explanation and captioned with what to notice. Flag screenshots dumped without interpretation.

## Book grammar
Course/type/scope/title should form a coherent chapter opening rather than an app toolbar. Major sections should be separated mostly through hierarchy and whitespace. Tables should resemble textbook tables; code should not become a visually dominant dark UI panel.

## Artifact-specific chrome
A summary or rapid review should read as a document without persistent sidebar navigation. A long guide may use a quiet editorial TOC and thin reading-progress line, but both must remain secondary to the paper.

## Editorial measure
Prose measure and figure/table width are different concerns. Ordinary paragraphs should remain around a comfortable book-like line length even when technical figures or tables use more of the page width.

## Responsive
Mobile must preserve reading order, avoid horizontal prose scrolling, and move gutter metadata above the associated content without leaving large empty spacer rows.

## Print
No clipped blocks, broken page flow, dark theme leakage or unnecessary navigation. Major headings should not strand at the bottom of a page.

## Accessibility
Check contrast, focus visibility, semantic labels, non-color meaning and reduced-motion behavior.

## Anti-slop checks
Fail or strongly penalize:
- gradients used only to make the page feel modern;
- fake paper textures, grain, notebook lines, stickers or vintage effects;
- strong drop shadows or floating-card styling;
- uniform rounded cards around every section;
- pill badges everywhere;
- huge hero taking a screen before useful content;
- decorative numbering that does not represent a real sequence;
- ornamental book cosplay: faux deckled paper, fake binding, flourishes, drop caps or vintage motifs without a learning function;
- excessive motion;
- visual novelty that competes with studying.
