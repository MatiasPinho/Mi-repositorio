# Learning principles

The output must help a student understand on first attentive reading.

Priority order:
1. build a useful mental model;
2. explain why the idea exists or what problem it solves;
3. connect it to prerequisites and neighboring concepts;
4. use a simple example where abstraction would otherwise be opaque;
5. choose a relevant visual representation when spatial/structural/process information would otherwise burden prose;
6. state the precise course formulation/conditions;
7. highlight traps and exam-relevant distinctions after comprehension exists.

Do not confuse precision with copying source language. A technically faithful explanation may be substantially clearer than the teacher's original wording.

A student-facing artifact fails if a generic “summarize these files for a student” prompt would produce something clearer and more natural.

Reading alone is not the whole learning loop. Student artifacts may contain sparse retrieval prompts that ask the learner to reconstruct, compare or explain without looking.
